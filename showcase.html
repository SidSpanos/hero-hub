The rule

Every skill scores at least 3

The good ones score 4. The ones that decide a fight score 5. Then skin and meta on top. That is the entire model — and it agrees with a hand-built tier list on 89% of hero pairs.

WORTH = 5 SKILLS + SKIN + META + YOUR CALL

Everyone sorts by it and the tiers fill in the sizes your board already uses, so the best score always lands in the best tier. A tie goes to the better kit — the five skills alone — because skin and meta are things about a hero, and the skills are the hero.

5
How the hero kills. An 8,000+ payload, a summon, or defence-down.
4
A real multiplier or real sustain. Damage-up 15%+, counter 10%+, healing, mitigation 10%+.
3
It does something, but it is small. A 3 has not been missed — it has been weighed.
+1
Each skill inside a trifecta. Three of a side is a trifecta, four a quadfecta.

Payload against defence ignored, all 37 plotted. The two multiply — a big hit into stripped armour lands for far more than the same hit into a full set — so the top-right corner is where any hero would want to be. It is empty except for one.

Kept honest

What it admits

A card that pretends is worse than a card that owns up. All four of these are on the face of the app, not buried in a comment.

Unread slots

A tooltip never opened scores nothing and the card says unread. Baldur sits on 8 because four of his five have never been shot — not because his kit is poor.

Blank is not zero

Power is recorded only at level 60 and 6 stars, so it is one yardstick. Short of that, a hero stays blank — and the card counts how many are actually in.

A ceiling, not a scale

Ignoring 50% of a target's defence and ignoring 7% both score 5, because 5 is the top. A separate card prints the real magnitudes beside it.

Not everything is read

67 of the 181 skills carry effects no marker looks at — rage, movement, proc chances — and fall to 3 by design. The model reads a short list of things that decide fights.
